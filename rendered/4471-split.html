Why one ticket swallowed them

Decision 0001 merges a report into an open ticket when its subject line matches, lowercased and stripped of punctuation. Nobody reads the body. During an incident morning that is the right trade, and the desk made it deliberately.

It cannot separate these problems, because the cause is not in the subject. The cause follows from the tenant’s locale and the booking form they run, and a reporter types neither. Two people can write the same sentence and be hitting different bugs. The desk’s own argued pairs show it:

5 reports read as a one-day move; filed under parser by tenant.

That table is not wrong. It counts the one-day reports under the cause their tenant implies and footnotes them, which is the written convention. But the footnote is the problem: those reports describe a date that moved by a single day, which neither the parser bug nor the hint bug can produce. A month flip moves 12 May to 5 December. These people are describing Tuesday showing as Monday, one client in Lisbon and one in Toronto getting different days for the same meeting, and an invite that arrived before the booking was made. That is a date rendered in the viewer’s timezone from a timestamp, not a date parsed in the wrong order.

The parser fix shipped. If reports like these keep arriving, this is why.

What one-day needs next

Start with how a stored date becomes a day for display, and in whose timezone — the tenant’s, the viewer’s, or UTC. The Lisbon and Toronto report is the one to reproduce first: one meeting, two readers, two different days.

Before you rely on this page
